## The currency

The Thai baht (THB, ฿) comes in notes of 20, 50, 100, 500 and 1,000 and coins of 1, 2, 5 and 10 baht. Check today's rate with the [currency converter](/essentials/#converter).

## Cash and cards

- Street food, markets, songthaews and small shops take **cash only**.
- Malls, hotels and chain restaurants accept Visa and Mastercard. Choose to pay in **baht** when the terminal asks. The "your currency" option uses a worse rate.
- Thai ATMs charge foreign cards about **220–250 THB per withdrawal**, on top of your own bank's fees. Withdraw larger amounts less often.

## Exchanging money

Exchange booths in cities usually beat airport rates. Bring your **passport**: it is required to exchange cash. Clean, undamaged notes get the best rate.

## QR payments

Thais pay for almost everything by scanning a **PromptPay** QR code. Some international wallets and travel cards can now pay Thai QR codes. Check with your provider before you rely on it.

## Tipping

Tipping is not expected but is appreciated. Rounding up a taxi fare or leaving 20–50 THB at a restaurant is normal. Many hotels and restaurants add a 10% service charge.

## VAT refund for tourists

You can claim back the 7% VAT on goods you take out of Thailand within 60 days when:

- you buy at shops showing the **"VAT Refund for Tourists"** sign,
- you spend at least 2,000 THB at one shop in one day and ask for form P.P.10, and
- your total is at least 5,000 THB.

Show the goods and forms at the VAT refund office at the airport **before** check-in.
